Will we hit enrollment? The odds, not a single date

Jane Natividad  ·  jane-natividad.github.io  ·  View on GitHub

Enrollment plans are usually a straight line: sites × rate × months. Real recruitment isn't. Sites open late, some never recruit, and the good ones are far better than average. This forecaster simulates site start-up and patient recruitment 2,000 times and tells you the probability of hitting your target by your deadline. Then it shows what each rescue lever, such as adding sites or opening them faster, actually does to those odds.

Model card

Model

Poisson–Gamma recruitment model, the standard approach in the clinical-operations forecasting literature (Anisimov & Fedorov, 2007). For each simulated trial: site i activates at a_i ~ Uniform(first, last activation month) site i is non-performing with probability p_np → rate λ_i = 0 otherwise λ_i ~ Gamma(shape k = 1/CV², mean = λ̄) (screened patients / site / month) randomizations in month t ~ Poisson( Σ_i λ_i · (1 − screen-fail) · exposure_i(t) ) where exposure is the fraction of month t that site i was open. Screen failure thins the Poisson process, so randomizations stay Poisson.

What the outputs mean

Odds of finishing by the deadline is the share of the 2,000 runs that reach the target by that month. P50/P80/P90 are the months by which 50%, 80% and 90% of runs have finished. ">36" means the target wasn't reached within the 36-month horizon. The band is the spread of simulated outcomes given these assumptions. It isn't a confidence interval on real-world performance.

Assumptions you supply

Every default, including rates, the activation window and the non-performer share, is illustrative. Nothing is drawn from a real trial or sponsor. The model assumes recruitment rates stay constant once a site is open, with no seasonality, saturation or site closures.

To calibrate it for real use

Fit the Gamma parameters to historical site-level recruitment from comparable trials. Replace the uniform activation window with actual start-up timelines. Then, once the trial is running, update the forecast with each site's observed recruitment, which is the Bayesian re-estimation step in the same literature.
"A straight-line enrollment plan has a hidden assumption: every site is average and on time. The simulation drops that assumption, and the first thing it usually shows is that the plan's finish date only has around even odds."
